StoryCam — how to record with both cameras

How to record the front and back camera at the same time

By Den · November 16, 2026

I was traveling and wanted one video that showed my face and the place at the same time — my reaction and the view, in the same frame, with no editing after. That is exactly what StoryCam does, and this is how to record it.

Record both cameras in four steps

  1. Open StoryCam and pick a layout. The place fills the screen and you appear in a small inset — picture-in-picture, circle, or split screen. Choose the one that fits the moment before you press record.
  2. Tap to swap, if you want it the other way round. One tap makes your face full-frame and puts the place in the inset instead. You can swap before recording; what you see on screen is exactly what gets saved.
  3. Press record — both cameras record together into one portrait video. There is no switching mid-take and no second file to stitch later. When you stop, you can press record again right away for the next take.
  4. Share it. After you stop, the share button opens the system share sheet, so the video goes straight to Instagram, WhatsApp, or wherever your family is — no export step, no gallery hunt.

Make it look like you meant it: filters

A plain walk can look flat. StoryCam has 8 filters on a slider — from clean to game-style looks, including Vice City and San Andreas — so the video looks finished before you send it. Pick the filter before you press record; it applies to the video as you record it.

Captions in Russian and English, on the phone

My family speaks Russian and English, so the video needs captions both of them can read. StoryCam adds captions on the phone itself, with the Whisper speech model — no account, no cloud, your audio never leaves the device. The caption models are about 160 MB and download on first use; the download and the captioning both run as system notifications with a progress bar and a Stop button, and the original video is always kept.

One honest note: auto-detection struggles with very short clips — a single word is the hardest case. If your clip is short, force the language (Русский or English) instead of relying on auto-detect, and you will get captions that actually match what you said.

The map is in the video, not just on screen

While you record, a small live map sits in the corner showing where you are. In version 0.5.15 the map is burned into the recorded video itself, so the video shows where it was taken even after you share it somewhere the location is stripped from the file.

Which phones can do this

Recording with both cameras at once needs an ARM64 Android phone with concurrent-camera support — Pixel 6 and newer, Galaxy S22 and newer work. If the phone cannot run both cameras at once, StoryCam falls back to single-camera recording instead of failing. That is the whole requirement: no account, no subscription, no paid version.
