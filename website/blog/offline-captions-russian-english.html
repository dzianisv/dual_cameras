StoryCam — captions, on the phone

Automatic captions on your videos, offline — in Russian and English

By Den · November 24, 2026

Most of the videos I send my family are watched on mute — on a bus, late at night, with the sound off. Captions fix that, but every caption tool I tried wanted the video uploaded to a server first. So StoryCam captions the video on the phone itself: Russian and English, no upload, no account, no internet needed after the first download.

Why on-device matters

Cloud captioning means your voice and your video leave the phone. On-device captioning means they never do. The speech model runs entirely on your phone, so the audio is never uploaded, never stored on a server, and never tied to an account — there is no account to tie it to. It also works where the internet does not: on a plane, in the mountains, on a beach with one bar of signal. Once the model is on the phone, captions work anywhere.

How it works, in practice

  1. Record your video as usual. Captions are a separate step after recording — nothing changes about how you film.
  2. Turn captions on and pick a language. Auto-detect guesses Russian or English for you, or force one of the two. The caption model (about 160 MB) downloads once, on first use, as a system notification with a progress bar and a Stop button that actually cancels.
  3. Wait for the job to finish. The captioning runs as a system notification too, so you can leave the app and come back. Your original video is always kept untouched.
  4. Get a captioned copy in your gallery. The captions are burned into a second copy of the video, with karaoke highlighting that follows the words as they are spoken. Share that copy — the original stays yours.

Russian and English, honestly

My family speaks both, so the captions have to work in both — not machine-translated, but transcribed in the language actually spoken. Auto-detection handles a normal conversation that stays in one language. Two honest limits: a single short word is the hardest case for auto-detection, so for short clips force the language instead of relying on the guess; and silence stays silent — captions only appear where speech is detected, so a quiet stretch of video does not get invented words.

What "offline" actually costs you

One download of about 160 MB, once, on first use. That is the whole price: no subscription, no per-minute fee, no account, no ads. After that the phone does the work, and the work is free. The model is verified before it is trusted, and if the download is interrupted you can stop it and start again — nothing half-downloaded gets used.

When this is the right tool

Use on-device captions when the video is personal — family, friends, a trip — and you do not want it on a server. Use them when you are offline or the connection is bad. Use them when the audience reads Russian, English, or both, and the video would otherwise be watched on mute. If you need ten languages or a transcript document, a cloud service is the better tool; StoryCam does two languages, on the phone, for free.

That is the whole guide. Record the video, turn captions on, share the captioned copy — your family reads what you said, even with the sound off.

Get StoryCam — free

StoryCam is live on Google Play (version 0.5.19), and the free APK is on GitHub. It needs an ARM64 Android phone with front-and-back camera support (Pixel 6 and newer, Galaxy S22 and newer work; other phones fall back to single-camera recording).
